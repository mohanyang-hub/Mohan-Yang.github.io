About

Profile

My research interests lie at the intersection of agricultural remote sensing, crop phenotyping, 3D scene reconstruction, and process-based simulation. I am particularly interested in how canopy structure, spectral response, microclimate, and disease development can be integrated to support precise crop monitoring and early warning.

Current work includes cucumber downy mildew monitoring in greenhouse environments and sugar beet Cercospora leaf spot analysis using multi-source observations, including RGB imagery, multispectral data, hyperspectral/SPAD measurements, 3D canopy structure, and environmental variables.

Research

Research Directions

Multi-source disease data acquisition

Building standardized datasets for different disease stages, cultivars, and growth periods by combining 3D structure, spectral imagery, microclimate, physiological parameters, and manual disease assessments.

3D multimodal disease monitoring

Fusing hyperspectral/SPAD observations, UAV or proximal multispectral imagery, RGB texture features, and 3D canopy parameters to identify disease severity at plant and canopy scales.

Canopy microenvironment risk prediction

Coupling realistic canopy structure with radiation, leaf temperature, humidity, and leaf wetness duration to quantify disease-risk patterns in greenhouse crop systems.

Helios / C++ / Greenhouse simulation

Greenhouse cucumber canopy microclimate and disease-risk simulation

This project loads cucumber canopy meshes, greenhouse wall and roof PLY files, assigns optical properties for greenhouse structures, and simulates radiation, leaf temperature, leaf relative humidity, wetness state, and daily disease-risk indicators. The workflow uses Helios plugins including radiation, energy balance, solar position, visualization, photosynthesis, stomatal conductance, and plant hydraulics.

  • Input geometry: cucumber canopy, greenhouse wall, and greenhouse roof PLY files.
  • Structure parameters: PAR reflectivity, PAR transmissivity, and longwave emissivity.
  • Outputs: PAR, leaf temperature, leaf humidity, wetness state, and disease-risk metrics.
  • Visualization: Helios Visualizer pseudocolor rendering for per-primitive simulation results.

LESS / Python / Hyperspectral simulation

Maize canopy hyperspectral radiative transfer simulation

This project uses LESS and pyLessSDK to construct a maize canopy scene and run batch hyperspectral radiative transfer simulations. The script imports maize stem and leaf OBJ files, places 28 crop instances in a structured canopy layout, assigns soil and leaf optical properties, and configures an orthographic spectral sensor from 400.50 nm to 899.50 nm.

  • Input data: maize stem and leaf OBJ geometry plus optical-property text files.
  • Scene design: regular maize canopy placement with object rotation for structural variation.
  • Sensor setup: 600 x 600 orthographic hyperspectral image simulation.
  • Batch workflow: updates optical properties and runs one LESS simulation for each input file.
